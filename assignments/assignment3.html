1. Why are so many companies using cloud-based email and calendar services?

Many companies are using cloud-based services because they can save money and reduce the workload for employees, which is especially useful for smaller companies. Companies do not have to buy and manage their own servers where the email system would run. Cloud service providers such as Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace operate the servers and take care of maintenance and updates. The company only needs to pay for the subscription. Cloud-based services also make it easier for a company to grow. If the company gets more employees, it does not need to buy additional hardware for its email system. Instead, it can simply add more user accounts to the service. Another advantage is that employees can access their email and calendars from different devices and locations, which is useful when people work remotely or travel for work.

2. What would a mid-size company store in Amazon Glacier? What would it not store?

A mid-size company could use Amazon Glacier to store old server backups, years-old database backups and other important documents that the company still needs to keep but does not use on a daily basis. For example, a company could store old financial records or backups from several years ago in Glacier. The reason for using Glacier for this type of data is that it is designed for long-term storage and archiving. The company can keep important data without using more expensive storage that is designed for frequently accessed files. However, a company would not normally store files that employees need to access every day in Glacier. This is because retrieving archived data can be slower and can also have additional costs.

3. When should a company maintain its own web server, and when should it use SaaS/PaaS/IaaS?

A company should consider using its own servers if it needs specialised hardware, complete control over its infrastructure or has special security requirements. It can also make sense if the company already has the necessary hardware and IT staff to maintain the servers. Cloud services can be a better choice for smaller companies because they may not have enough money or IT staff to build and maintain their own server infrastructure. Cloud services are also useful when the company's applications do not require specialised hardware or unusual infrastructure. For example, a small company that needs to host a normal website may find it easier to use a cloud provider instead of buying and maintaining a physical server. In my opinion, the choice depends on what the company needs. Running your own servers gives you more control, but it also means more work and responsibility. Cloud services usually require less maintenance and can be easier to scale as the company grows.

4.Describe reasons, why many IoT devices (internet connected home appliances, Smart-TVs and such) are using Google's 8.8.8.8 or some other public DNS resolvers?

The manufacturers don't know what type of network the device will be connected to, so instead of relying completely on the local network's DNS configuration, the manufacturer can configure a known public DNS resolver such as Google's 8.8.8.8. This makes it easier for the device to work on different networks and with different Internet service providers. It also means that the user does not have to manually configure the DNS settings for the device. This can be useful for devices such as Smart-TVs because they need to connect to online services such as Netflix, software update servers and the manufacturer's own services. Another reason for using public DNS is that large companies such as Google have DNS servers that are designed to be reliable and available around the world. However, there are also some disadvantages. When a device uses a public DNS resolver, the DNS provider can see the DNS requests made by the device, which can create privacy concerns. It can also bypass some DNS-based filtering that has been configured on the home network.
